Life on Mango Street

My name is Esperanza Cordero, and this is my blog. I live in a house on Mango Street. I hope you enjoy my stories.

I Hate My Name - March 17, 1967

I can’t stand my name. I wish I was named something else or at least born somewhere else—someplace where my name wouldn’t be made fun of by the other kids. I know that my mother named me after my great-grandmother who was born in the year of the horse, just like I was. Even then, I can’t help but hate my name. Maybe if I had been named something pretty like Sofia, the kids at my school wouldn’t make fun of my name. They make fun of my name, calling me Ess-ppur-uhn-za, stuttering on purpose when saying my name. I guess I’ll just have to wait until I’m older to change it.

My Friend Alicia - April 3, 1967

There’s a girl on my street. Her name is Alicia. She’s always busy doing something whether it’s cooking, cleaning, or studying ever since her mom died. She always tries to do her best, but I can tell that she is tired. She’s physically tired, but also tired of living the way she is living now. I can see on her face that she wants to escape and live a better life—a life away from all the mice she says that she sees. She doesn’t want to become trapped in a factory for the rest of her life, like her mother before her. She wants to be free like a bird. She wants to take the risk of studying in hopes of getting a better job. Others aren’t so lucky. They take on the burdens of their parents as they grow older, working in factories or stuck at home. I hope she can break through the expectations placed on her.

Today We Got Pretty Shoes - July 23, 1967

Today, we got a bag of shoes—real pretty shoes. Me, Rachel, and Lucy tried them on; we tried standing, we tried walking. Rachel showed us the ropes. We stood, we walked, we ran. We walked all along the street, down past Mr. Benny’s old shop where it always smelled like cigar smoke; I could just feel the stares of jealous girls as we walked by. Then a creepy old man started calling out to us, telling Rachel that she is “so pretty with those lemon shoes. Lucy and I grabbed Rachel and ran as fast as our legs would take us and ran all the way back, back to the safety of Mango Street. We hid those shoes, and they’re still hidden. Hidden underneath the bushel basket on the back porch. I loved being beautiful for a while, but good things can’t last forever, especially not with people like that bum man catcalling girls as young as us. I wonder why the world is this way. “We are tired of being beautiful.”
